Executive Summary

Strong Period · Phones Still Lagging

This was the strongest month on record for lead volume: 106 paid leads on $20.6K of spend, and a blended cost per lead of $194 — the lowest reading yet, edging past the $204 posted last fortnight even as the window doubled in length. Meta did the heavy lifting with 64 leads at $136.19 each, led by Remarketing at $46.85 and Brochure Download at $87.05. Google delivered 42 conversions at $282, and the two campaigns that spent $2,124 with nothing to show last period — Search_Competitors and Key Feeder Markets — are now converting, at 5 and 5.5 respectively; Display added 14 conversions at $110.88, though most of its phone leads were anonymous callers.

The site itself was flat: Looker's like-for-like read puts active users at +0.7% and views at −2.7%. Search impressions grew 23.2%, but average position slipped from 14.7 to 16.0, largely on wrong-city Miramar, Florida apartment queries that earn impressions and no clicks. The phone is the caveat that persists. 26 calls came in and 14 were answered — a 54% answer rate, up from 33% but still under the 70% floor — with 12 missed and none tagged as leads. Pipeline is moving the right way: the warm bench grew from 7 to 19, sales visits doubled from 4 to 8, reservations held at 2, and there are still zero hot leads. Two new channels also appeared this period — a Wall Street Journal preseason-2026 placement drove 1,267 sessions and wfla CTV/addressable display in New York drove 207 — but neither has cost or conversion data in the dashboard yet. Cheap leads are arriving in volume; the job now is answering the phone and converting the bench.

Website & Organic

Traffic Flat · Position Slipping

Traffic is flat on a like-for-like basis. The growth figures in this section are Looker's own comparison of these 31 days against the preceding 31 (Jul 14 – Aug 13), not a comparison to the 15-day prior report, so they are clean: 10.1K active users (+0.7%), 17.6K views (−2.7%) and 1.7 pages per user (−3.4%). Paid channels supply two-thirds of visits — paid social 33.3% and paid search 31.7% — with direct at 22.2% and organic search at 6.3%. Google sessions totaled 4,635 (1,950 of them from Display placements) and Meta 2,160. Two channels appeared for the first time: a Wall Street Journal preseason-2026 placement drove 1,267 sessions, the third-largest source, and wfla CTV / addressable display in New York drove 207. Organic went the wrong way: average position worsened from 14.7 to 16.0 while impressions rose 23.2% to 13.9K and queries edged up 2.1% to 336 — the impression growth is largely hollow, with at least 1,656 impressions from wrong-city Miramar, Florida searches earning zero clicks. Branded search is solid: "mira mar sarasota" alone drew 2,252 impressions and 43 clicks. The deep-intent pages still hold visitors — Broker Toolkit 3:17 per visit, Our History 2:41, and the Residences page at a 7.99% bounce with 2:04 on page.

Lead Pipeline & Phone

Pipeline Up · Phones Weak

Pipeline is moving the right way. The Spark warm bench grew from 7 to 19, sales visits or meetings doubled from 4 to 8, reservation holders held at 2, and there are still zero hot leads. Those four figures are pipeline stock at the time of extraction, not in-period flow; the in-period rows in the Spark Digital Ads Leads table number 14 — 7 Warm and 7 Agent. The 7 warm arrivals split Google Ads 4 (Non-Brand Sarasota 2, Key Feeder Markets 1, Competitors 1) and Meta 3 (Instagram prospecting 2, Facebook Key Feeder Market 1). The phone is better but still short: 26 calls came in over the 31 days and 14 were answered, a 54% answer rate against 33% last period but under the 70% floor. 12 calls were missed and none of the 26 was tagged as a lead. 14 of the 31 days saw no calls at all. Google Ads drove 16 of the 26 calls, so every missed one was paid for. Call volume genuinely doubled on a per-day basis, from 0.40 to 0.84 a day; average call duration was 58.5 seconds.
