Executive Summary

Cheapest Leads of the Year · Follow-Up Lagging

The media buy got noticeably more efficient this fortnight: spend eased to $9.2K while lead volume rose to 45, pulling blended cost per lead down 25% to $204 — the best reading of the year. Meta carried that result with 30 leads at $122.75 each, and the Brochure Download creative produced 10 leads at just $69.68. Google's search side improved its cost per lead 23% to $368, though the account still converts at 0.97% and the Competitors and Key Feeder Markets campaigns spent $2,124 between them without a single tracked conversion. The concern is upstream of the ads. Site traffic fell sharply — visitors down roughly a third to 3.7K as Display pulled back — and organic average position slipped from 12.9 to 14.7, giving back last period's gain. The phone went quiet and the coverage problem returned: 6 calls, 3 answered, a 50% answer rate after last fortnight's 88%. Pipeline quality is the other flag — zero hot leads, two reservation holders holding steady, and a warm bench of 7. In short, we are buying leads more cheaply than at any point this year, and the work now is making sure those leads get answered and worked.

Budget & ROI

Efficient Spend

More leads on less money — the efficiency story of the period. Spend came down 11% to $9,201 while leads rose 18% to 45, taking blended cost per lead from $273 to $204, the lowest of the year. Google's cost per lead improved 23% to $368 and Meta's fell 20% to $123, back under the $150 target. The efficient lines are familiar: Meta Brochure Download ($70), Meta Remarketing ($97) and Branded Search ($103) anchor the account. The two problems are at the other end — Meta Prospecting Sarasota returned one website lead for $718, and two Google campaigns spent $2,124 with no tracked conversion at all, so no cost per lead can even be calculated for them.

Google Ads

Cheaper Leads · Two Campaigns Idle

Google delivered 15 conversions — 12 website form submissions and 2 phone-call leads — at $368 each, a 23% improvement. Non-Brand Sarasota did the work, converting 10 times on 658 clicks at an 8.11% click-through, and Branded Search remains the cheapest line in the account at $103 per conversion off a 41% click-through. The blended conversion rate reads 0.97% (search-only 1.13%), and the improvement over last period's 0.41% mostly reflects Display scaling back rather than search getting better. Two campaigns are the problem: Competitors spent $1,320.93 and Key Feeder Markets $802.89 with zero tracked conversions between them. Cost per click rose to $3.61. Conversions now come two-thirds from mobile, and clicks still skew 55–64 — the affluent buyer profile the project is built for.

Meta Ads

The Efficiency Engine

Meta is the standout. On essentially flat spend of $3,682 it produced 30 leads at $122.75 each — a 25% lift in volume and a 20% improvement in cost per lead, back under the $150 target. Brochure Download remains the cheapest reliable source in the entire program: 10 leads at $69.68 from a single static building image with no video at all. Remarketing came in at $97.26 on three leads, and Feeder Market Segmented carried the volume with 12 leads at $165. Video is doing reach rather than forms — Video_Sarasota Lifestyle drew 10.1K views and 4,778 engagements on Feeder Market. The one line to cut is Prospecting Sarasota, which spent $718.45 for a single website lead. Click-through improved to 1.76%.

Website & Organic

Traffic Decline

Traffic contracted materially. Site visitors fell roughly a third to 3.7K and views dropped by more than half to 6.4K — almost entirely because Display pulled back from 1.23M impressions to 189K, taking display_placement_feedermarket from 2,003 sessions down to 381. Strip Display out and the core held up reasonably: Google non-branded search delivered 645 sessions and Meta was steady at 1,062. Pages per user eased only slightly to 1.7, so the audience is smaller but not meaningfully less engaged. Organic went the wrong way: average position slipped from 12.9 to 14.7, giving back last period's gain, while query volume was flat at 211 and impressions actually grew 7% to 4.8K. The deep-intent pages still perform — Broker Toolkit averaged 4:28 per visit and the Residences page held a 6.6% bounce at 2:09.

Lead Pipeline & Phone

Phone Coverage Regressed

The phone problem is back. Only 6 calls came in this fortnight and 3 were answered — a 50% answer rate against 88% last period, reversing the coverage fix reported then. None of the six were tagged as leads, and two of the missed calls came from paid Google Ads clicks the program paid for. Volume was healthy on the digital side: 42 new leads registered in the period, 30 from Meta and 12 from Google. But the pipeline quality flags are real — zero hot leads, a warm bench reading 7 against 23 a fortnight ago, and 2 reservation holders holding steady with 4 sales visits or meetings in motion. Seven named warm prospects are still live, several dating back to December through April — Anthony Koblinski, Steve Shuhet, Cameron Knuth and Vlad Nesterenco are all long-lead Google search leads worth a direct touch. Agent activity was notable too, with Kelly Dunlop (Coldwell Banker Lakewood Ranch) requesting a showing and Linda Scheller meeting the team at RASM.
